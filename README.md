# Metropolis Ascendant

*One metropolis. Every victory. — a tall overhaul for Sid Meier's Civilization VII.*

Most of Civ VII rewards going **wide** — more settlements, more of everything. Metropolis Ascendant flips that:
you build few cities and pour everything into making them extraordinary — a dense, ever-growing metropolis that
out-thinks, out-builds and out-cultures empires many times its size.

It began as a tall overhaul that layered bonuses onto the game's own trees. It has grown into **a tall
civilization system of its own** — a custom civic tree, research boosts, earned feats, purpose-written cards and
pantheons, and a live dashboard tying it together. Your bonuses run at full strength only while your footprint
stays small, and switch off the moment you overreach.

## The Ascendancy Tree

Your metropolis researches its **own civic tree** — three roots, deepening masteries, and a repeatable capstone,
re-keyed to every Age. Route toward Science, Culture, Economy, Expansion, Military or Diplomacy and your city
specializes accordingly. Bonuses pay out per Urban Population and deepen through each node's mastery; every
node's panel tells you exactly what it grants.

## Civic Boosts

Every civic on the Ascendancy tree carries a **Boost** — a goal shown right on the node, in the spirit of
Civilization VI's tech and civic boosts. Steer your play toward it and that civic researches faster. Not a
freebie — a reward for playing with intent. Line your boosts up and you can race down a path well ahead of
schedule. Boosted nodes are flagged on the tree and in the dashboard.

## Feats & Dedications

- **Fifteen Major feats** mark the achievements of a towering city, each earning a Triumph. **Rewards are
  granted the moment the Triumph fires** — a Tradition with an extra slot to carry it, a named bonus package
  (Lyceum, Sacred Precinct, …), an immediate +1 settlement allowance, or a next-Age Dedication. The Triumph text states the
  exact effects, numbers and all — no hidden trees, no second research step.
- **Settlement allowance** — each Age’s Expansion feat raises it by 1, directly: the deliberate way to widen your
  allowance. You begin with a single city and earn your way toward more — **never past four**. Every bonus pays
  across all of your settlements, and switches off the instant you exceed what you've earned. Never map luck;
  always something you earned.
- **Dedications** carry your legacy across the Age transition — your metropolis begins the next era already ahead.

### Every settlement is a decision

With so few settlements, **where and when you place each one is a strategic choice with real trade-offs** — the
allowance tells you how many; you decide what they're for.

- **The Antiquity slot (Settlers' Charter) is pure tempo.** Its value is a second city for the rest of Antiquity —
  by Exploration your allowance is 2 regardless. Spend it or skip it; skipping is a legitimate tall opening.
- **Spend it at home, and Exploration sends the bill.** Antiquity has no Distant Lands, so both your settlements
  enter Exploration ashore — and **Twin Capitals** (Exploration's Expansion feat: grow a Distant Lands settlement
  into a full City to earn the Royal Charter and your third slot) needs exactly what you no longer have room for.
  Founding the Distant Lands town takes you one over your allowance: your yield bonuses suspend until the moment
  that town becomes a City and Twin Capitals restores you. **Bank the Gold to convert it immediately and the dip
  lasts a turn or two.** (Your Great Work slots, unlocks and Suzerain streams hold through the dip; feat progress
  never stops.)
- **Hold the slot, and Exploration opens clean.** Your second settlement goes straight to the Distant Lands, Twin
  Capitals fires at a legal two, and the earned third can go wherever you want — including a **second Distant
  Lands port**, which doubles your treasure-fleet sources for the Economic legacy path.
- The same logic runs through Modern: the carried third plus the fourth from **The Contented Empire** (Modern's
  Expansion feat: every settlement Ecstatic at once) are your only pieces — and a Distant Lands settlement grown
  to 25 population is itself a Modern feat, so a slot invested there early keeps paying achievements later.

## Traditions

A set of custom **Traditions** you slot into your government, written for one dense city — from steady
per-population yields to sharp tradeoffs like *Forges Before Fields* (more Production, less Food). Everything the
mod adds is a Tradition; there are no separate Policies. The bigger Traditions you earn from your **feats** carry
across the Age transition and stack, so your metropolis compounds instead of starting over.

## Custom Pantheons

Two beliefs you won't find in the base game, each with its own hand-made icon, chosen from the same list as
every other pantheon:

- **Goddess of the Plantation** — +1 Culture on Plantations.
- **Mistress of the Beasts** — +1 Happiness and +1 Production on Pastures and Camps.

## The tall engine underneath

- **Urban Population is power.** Your city's Urban Population (not rural) drives everything. Specialists pay out
  full tile adjacency, so a dense, well-placed city compounds fast.
- **Suzerain layer.** Bonuses scale with how many city-states you're Suzerain of — your substitute for going wide.
- **Happiness pays off.** A metropolis that runs **Joyous** (and then **Ecstatic**) earns escalating Science and
  Culture per Urban Population, and its specialists cost less to maintain until the city is thriving — turning
  the staged-happiness system into a tall-exclusive strength.
- **Wonders & Arcadia.** Concentrate Wonders and natural beauty in one city — a strength the AI never builds
  toward. Buildings next to a Wonder are happier and Wonders raise the surrounding **Appeal**; discover a Natural
  Wonder and Arcadia opens: your **Breathtaking** rural tiles, Buildings beside **Mountains** and even the peaks
  themselves become productive, and the waters pay too — rivers, lakes, the open ocean, reefs and water Natural
  Wonders each yield by type, growing richer each Age. A water-heavy or island map is never a dead end.
- **Reach beyond your borders.** Build a **Surveyor** to claim a resource tile up to 5 tiles from your Settlements
  into your metropolis (spent after one claim), so one dense city can command resources a wide empire would spread
  across many.
- **Raze what you can't hold.** A dense city can't keep its conquests, so razing becomes a real tool: take a city
  by force for a one-time burst of **Gold** and **Influence**, pillage its buildings for more, and raze it in a
  turn or two instead of many. Tied to the tall playstyle — a conquer-and-keep empire gains none of it.
- **Every victory stays open.** When a packed metropolis has no open tile for a Modern victory Wonder, raise its
  **Foundations** over an existing district and it completes into the World's Fair or Manhattan Project in place —
  available only once you've earned that Wonder normally, so it's pure tile relief, never a shortcut. All victory
  paths stay reachable in a dense city.

## The Bonus Dashboard

Every Metropolis Ascendant bonus for the current Age in one read-only panel — open it from its button in the
sub-system dock:

- **What's active, what's next.** Every bonus grouped by focus: lit when unlocked, grey with the node that
  unlocks it when not — with All / Active / Locked views and collapsible lanes.
- **Live figures** (optional companion mod). Install
  [leonardfactory's Policy Yields Preview](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3515801789)
  and every bonus shows a computed per-turn figure, plus a headline "Metropolis Ascendant right now" total.
  Without it the dashboard shows a clean catalog view — nothing breaks, you just see no numbers.
- **Ascendancy & footprint.** Your Ascendancy tree and mastery progress, the Settlements-vs-allowance track with
  earned-slot pips, and which civics are boosted.
- **Vitals at a glance.** Your tall status — with a clear **SUSPENDED** banner if you exceed your earned
  settlement allowance — plus your Urban Population and your metropolis's happiness stage.
- **Working sections.** The Surveyor section counts claimable resources within reach (4–5 hexes out), and the
  Conquest section shows the current Age's real capture payouts.

## Built in the open

Metropolis Ascendant took Civ VII modding into places it hadn't gone — a full custom civic tree that renders,
researches and reveals its own effects; pantheons with bespoke art; Civ VI-style research boosts drawn onto the
live tree; a built-in dashboard reading the game's own yield attribution; custom units and UI. Every technique is
written up in an **[open Claude Code modding skill](https://github.com/chrislittle/civ7-modding-skill)** so the
community can build on what this mod discovered.

Every bonus is also documented in-game on the node that unlocks it. The complete breakdown — every bonus, the
node it gates on, and its tree depth — is in **[docs/bonus-list.md](docs/bonus-list.md)**.

## Install

**Steam Workshop:** [subscribe here](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3750473414), then enable **Metropolis Ascendant** in *Add-Ons* and start a new game.

**Manual:** copy this folder into your Mods directory, then enable it in *Add-Ons*:

```
%LOCALAPPDATA%\Firaxis Games\Sid Meier's Civilization VII\Mods\metropolis-ascendant
```

## Build from source

Everything under `data/` and `text/` is **generated** from config tables at the top of two PowerShell scripts
(PowerShell 7):

- `tools/gen-ascendant.ps1` — the **tall engine**: Urban-Population scaling, Suzerain, Wonders & Arcadia,
  Surveyor, Razing, Foundations, plus the README tiers table and the pillar sections of `docs/bonus-list.md`.
- `tools/gen-ascendancy.ps1` — the **Ascendancy civic tree**: nodes, masteries, civic boosts, feats/allowance slots,
  cards and pantheons; it also injects the Ascendancy sections into `docs/bonus-list.md`.

Edit the config, then run **both, in this order** (the second injects its bonus-list sections into the file the
first writes):

```powershell
& '.\tools\gen-ascendant.ps1'
& '.\tools\gen-ascendancy.ps1'
```

Each writes its `data/*` XML and generated notes, and self-validates the XML. `publish.ps1` runs both in this
order automatically. Paths are derived from the script location — no setup required.
